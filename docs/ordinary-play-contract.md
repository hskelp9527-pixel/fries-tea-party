# Ordinary play comes first

The latest user instruction supersedes the earlier decision to make three new
puzzles mandatory. Standard play follows the original core: enter the tea party,
understand that three watch gears are missing, explore, change size with tea/cake,
recover each size-specific gear, repair the watch, tap the highlighted cups,
reach 6:01, see the rose path, and replay. The short gear challenges are part of this flow
(`?nochallenges` skips them) and must stay completable.

The normal interface defaults to Simplified Chinese, with English voices. One
language preference owns title, hints, objectives, prompts, dialogue, settings,
results and text painted on readable props. The initial objective and 0/3 count
are visible immediately. Wrong-size approaches explain the obstruction. Hints
must advance when needed and not claim already-found gears are still missing.

The cottage must be an actual connected home: enter from the garden through the
door, circulate through a sitting area, kitchen/work area, sleeping and storage
space, collect the mantel gear, and leave through the same door. Walls, ceiling,
floors, room openings, camera and collisions must agree. Merely enlarging a
fireplace display is insufficient.

Every chair uses normal time. The player remains seated until choosing to stand
with E or movement. Repeated interactions shorten dialogue or use authored short
actions; they never speed up the global simulation or detach bone time from
movement time.

Acceptance needs an unskipped keyboard/controller playthrough and independent
review. Guided routes, debug placement, image counts and performance figures
remain specific diagnostics and cannot replace first-time comprehension or
visual checks. After this baseline passes, address the remaining contact,
facial animation, path/arch, lighting and composition rejection findings.
